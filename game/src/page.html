          How to execute it

          Keep building the 2D, single-player game in small phases, and test each phase on real players before starting the next. This path gives you something playable every week and keeps online features open for later.

          EnginePhaser 3With TypeScript and Vite. Runs in any modern browser.
          First releaseSingle-playerSaves stay in the browser, so you need no server.
          HostingStatic siteitch.io for playtests, then Cloudflare Pages or similar.
          My time estimate6 to 8 monthsOne developer, about 15 hours a week.

          The roadmap

          Each phase ends with a test. If the test fails, fix it before adding anything. These durations are my estimates and depend heavily on your experience and art plan.

          1. 0

            Prototype This page

            1 to 2 weeks

            Prove the combat, training and loot loop, with code-drawn pixel characters and your art on the select screen.

            Exit testFive minutes of play feels good with no real art.
          2. 1

            Vertical slice

            4 to 6 weeks

            Move to Phaser. Draw final sprite sheets for three of your characters. Add tile maps and one finished city district with its boss.

            Exit testA stranger keeps playing for 20 minutes without being asked to.
          3. 2

            Systems

            8 to 10 weeks

            Deepen skills and add a stash, crafting, and NPCs as vendors and quest givers. Keep characters, items and monsters in data files.

            Exit testYou can add a new item or enemy without changing code.
          4. 3

            Content

            8 to 12 weeks

            Finish sprite sheets for more of the 40 characters and draw frame-by-frame boss animation if playtests call for it. Add 10 distinct districts, music, sound and story.

            Exit testA first playthrough lasts 4 to 6 hours, and players start a second character.
          5. 4

            Playtest and launch

            4 to 6 weeks

            Release a public demo, collect crash and balance data, tune performance on cheap laptops, then launch.

            Exit testA steady 60 frames per second on a five-year-old laptop, with no save-loss bugs open.

          Choosing the engine

          Phaser fits best because it already includes the parts an action RPG needs and has no install step for players.

          OptionBest forMain drawbackVerdict
          Phaser 32D games with sprites, tile maps, physics and audioVery large battles need care to stay smoothStart here
          PixiJSFast 2D drawing with your own game codeYou write collisions, scenes and audio yourselfOnly if Phaser limits you
          Babylon.js or Three.jsA true 3D game in the browser3D art costs far more, and load times are longerOnly if 3D is essential
          Godot web exportVisual editor, and desktop releases laterLarger downloads and slower start in a browserGood if web is not your main target

          How the code fits together

          Keep the game rules separate from the drawing code. You can then test balance without a browser, and adding online play later will not require a rewrite.

          InputKeyboard, mouse, touch
          →
          SimulationCombat, loot and AI at a fixed tick rate
          →
          RendererPhaser sprites, effects, HUD
          Data filesItems, skills, enemies
          →
          Same simulationReads data, writes state
          →
          SavesIndexedDB, cloud later

          A starting structure for phase 1.

  data/       items.json  skills.json  enemies.json
  save/       versioned save files

          Start on your own machine

          You need Node.js installed. Run these three commands, then open the local address that the last one prints.

          npm create vite@latest dayrim -- --template vanilla-ts
          cd dayrim && npm install phaser
          npm run dev

          What the preview covers

          Included: 40 playable characters with their own stats, six action methods, 23 fixed-power weapons to collect to swap freely, full-body pixel animation, procedural overgrown maps with day and night, killable NPCs with gore and situational dialogue, weak and strong minions, rage-powered fullscreen ultimates, kill combos with voice-over, 10 stages with animated monster bosses, skills, gym training, potions, pills and automatic saving.

          Left out: final hand-drawn sprite sheets, unique districts, crafting, quests, story, music and online play. Characters in play are simplified pixel versions of your art.

          Where these projects stall

          A 40-character roster. Launch with three to five fully animated characters, then add more once the game is fun.

          Online play too early. It can double the work, and it needs a server and cheat protection. Save it for after launch.

          Animation. The code-drawn characters prove the game cheaply. Final sprite sheets in your art style remain the largest art cost, so test the price on three characters first.

          Four decisions for you

          These change the plan the most. My default is in green.

          Solo or online?

          Solo first. Online co-op can follow with a Node.js game server such as Colyseus.

          2D or 3D?

          2D. Choose 3D only if the look is central to your idea and you have the art budget.

          Keep the code-drawn pixels or go further?

          Keep them while testing, then commission sprite sheets for three characters to learn the real cost before committing to all 40.

          How will it make money?

          Free demo, then a one-time purchase. Avoid pay-to-win loot, and decide early if you want ads or cosmetics.

          Hero and villain cards are your Dayrim art as drawn. Monsters are your art cut out automatically, so some edges are rough. In-game characters, citizens, the city and effects are drawn by code. Your saved run is stored only in this browser.
